One pipeline, three entry points

From an idea to a signed-off film

Three content lines share the same back half. Adding a new source type means writing a brief contract and a planning prompt — rendering, QA, audio and assembly stay untouched.

01

Input

A topic line, a paper PDF, or a repository README. Brief validators reject missing evidence.

SCENE-WRITER PAPER2VIDEO PROJECT2VIDEO

02

Plan

SCENE-PLAN.json passes 11 hard checks: beat grid, density budget, off-centre composition, subtitle bounds.

03

Render

Reusable motion blocks assemble into one HTML file; headless Chrome captures deterministic frames.

04

Gate

11 metrics scored 1–10. Anything under 8 stops the line with exit 1 — the model never grades its own work.

05

Ship

BGM + SFX + TTS with side-chain ducking, ffmpeg assembly, and a machine-readable run report.

Quality is a number, not an opinion

The gate that refuses to ship

A language model will say a frame looks fine while a caption is cut off. Our checker measures the pixels instead, and the pipeline treats its verdict as law.

✓Motion density & still runs
✓Hard-cut rhythm
✓Style-aware safe area (v4)
✓Subtitle band coverage
✓Texture energy & composition
✓Accent focus & occupancy
✓Deterministic render (same t, same pixels)
✓CI regression fixtures

QA v4 detects content bleeding into the safe area relative to the film's own background — dark films catch bright spill, light films catch dark spill. An absolute brightness line was blind to both.

Straight answers

Questions we usually get

Why not Remotion, Motion Canvas, or an editor?

The engine was never the bottleneck. The gap is frame density, gate enforcement and iteration speed. A single canvas render path plus a pixel-level scorer makes aesthetics measurable and mistakes predictable.

Do you support vertical / short-form formats?

No. We tested it and deliberately dropped it: 1920×1080 landscape only. We state this as a boundary, not as a roadmap promise.

Is the pipeline locked to one model?

No. Rendering, QA, audio and assembly are model-independent. Planning defaults to a low-cost model; creative frames can use a stronger one. SCENE-PLAN.json is a plain JSON contract.

Does it need a GPU?

No. Our own development machine has no discrete GPU: narration uses edge-tts, frames are captured by headless Chrome over CDP, and audio is synthesised deterministically with NumPy.

What is actually verified, and what is not?

The engineering chain is tested end to end: deterministic frames, QA gate, three audio layers, assembly, run reports and CI fixtures. Some methodology rules come from observation rather than controlled A/B tests — we say so in the docs instead of overclaiming.
